# Every paper experiment reports the same five metrics, measured on a temperature ladder

## Context & Decision

The paper is a series of ablations — [shared backbone](../experiments/backbone.md), [auxiliary prediction heads](../experiments/aux.md), the [control clone](../experiments/clone.md), [control attention](../experiments/attention.md), and a [comparison against outside methods](../experiments/baselines.md). The first three differ in what they switch off, not in what they measure: each asks whether its condition changes *task performance, what its chosen design is ultimately worth, how far control generalizes, whether the generator's judgement generalizes with it, and how much exploration happens*. Fixing those five once is what makes the experiments a series rather than unrelated studies, and it is a contract — changing a metric after runs means re-running every experiment that used it. Formulas live in [`Metrics.md`](../reference/Metrics.md); terms in [`experiments/CONTEXT.md`](../../experiments/CONTEXT.md).

**This ADR governs experiments 1, 2 and 3.** Experiments 4 and 5 have no generator to measure — 4 by design, 5 in its baselines — and are covered by their own doc and by [ADR-0022](0022-cross-method-comparison-protocol.md) respectively; see the Consequences. Experiment names here are the mechanism names, not the paper's shorthand: what the paper calls *JEPA* is FD+FK ablated together, and what it calls *PPG* is the resample-time control clone, since codesign control is combined PPO (ADR-0013).

All three share one x-axis, the **resample window index**, with every window plotted and the pretrain→RL boundary marked. Eight seeds per condition, one full slot wave, read against the study's noise floor per [ADR-0018](0018-noise-floor-first-tuning.md). The budget is **48 windows** (8 pretrain, 40 RL), fixed in windows rather than in frames or epochs so every condition lands on the same axis — roughly double the tuner's trial budget, bought for metric 4, whose cumulative curves are read by their slope.

**The budget is a whole number of windows, and the epoch count is derived from it.** A window's metrics are all written by the resample that *closes* it, so a budget stopping mid-window silently discards that window: at the shipped `resample_interval: 1`, `max_episode_length: 1000` and `horizon_length: 16` a window spans `ceil(1000/16) = 63` epochs, so 48 windows is **3024 epochs** and the round 3000 would have given 47 windows plus 39 orphan epochs — metric 1 ending at w=46 while this ADR promises w=47. `harness/launch.py` derives both the epoch count and the checkpoint cadence from that arithmetic rather than restating either.

**1. Task performance is `quality/R_mean`, the window average.** Already logged, already phase-aligned per [ADR-0020](0020-composite-tuning-objective-with-collapse-gate.md). Two properties are accepted deliberately. It is a **joint body × control score** — measured on the generator's own bodies, so a run that collapses onto one easy body can beat one that keeps exploring; that is the honest "which method ends up better" number, and metrics 2–4 are what decompose it. And the window *average* charges each method for its own post-resample re-adaptation, rather than the recovered level, because for the PPG and shared-backbone conditions that dip is the thing under test: clone-KL exists to preserve control across a resample, and the shared backbone is what lets control transfer between bodies at all.

**5 is the specialized return, and it exists because metric 1 punishes generalization.** A method whose control policy stayed broad is, in the return curve, charged for never having specialized to any one body — so the very property the paper argues for reads as weaker performance. Metric 5 removes that: at each ladder checkpoint the codesign scaffolding is stripped (`resample_interval: 0`, aux heads off, generator discarded), every env carries the committed body, and control is fine-tuned 250 epochs on it alone. That budget is chosen to *approach* the fixed-body ceiling rather than to avoid it — the intent is to collapse control into a specialist, so what remains being compared is the **designs**, not the policies. It is plotted as markers on metric 1's axes, and it reuses the ladder's checkpoints and its level-0 body, so it costs no new machinery.

**2 and 3 are measured on a spread ladder**, a one-parameter family that divides the generator's masked logits by a temperature. Its landmarks are the body sources `eval.py` already has — greedy, stochastic, uniform — so the ladder is that three-point comparison filled in, and its top end is set by the grammar rather than by the run, making every condition's ladder comparable end to end. Levels are indexed by **mean `d_struct` from the committed body**, found by bisecting the temperature, so the axis reads in modules and is identical across seeds without interpolation. Run at three checkpoints (pretrain→RL boundary, mid-RL, final), so generalization *narrowing* as the generator commits is visible rather than inferred from an endpoint.

Two things the ladder gets for free. At level 0 every sampled body is the identical committed body, so the across-body spread there is **pure measurement noise** — the metric's own noise floor, measured in-band, per seed. And the same anchor fixes metric 3's confound: GenCrit regresses returns collected under the *sampled* policy by *earlier, weaker* control policies, while the ladder rolls out the final policy at μ. Both mismatches inflate apparent under-prediction, and neither is constant across conditions — the μ-vs-sampled gap scales with policy noise, which the PPG and backbone ablations change, and the staleness gap scales with how fast control improved, which experiment 1 measures. Level 0 is the generator's own mode, so `bias(0)` *is* the in-distribution offset; **excess bias** subtracts it and both confounds go with it.

**3 is a predicted-vs-actual overlay, not a correlation.** The actual line is metric 2's curve, so the two collapse into one figure and the gap between the lines is readable directly.

**4 is breadth *and* travel.** `build/n_modes` is a snapshot and cannot see exploration on its own: a generator holding one design that marches across design space reads as total collapse, and one holding the same three designs for the whole run reads as healthy. Travel is the window-to-window **energy distance** on the subtype-collapsed skeleton — mean cross-window `d_struct` with each window's own breadth subtracted — whose same-distribution null is measured by splitting a window's sample in half rather than assumed. **Mode coverage** is the cumulative companion, whose slope is the discovery rate.

**Runs dump their per-window generator population.** One npz per resample, written at the boundary *before* the next sample, where the closing window's trace and its `R` are both in hand — which sidesteps the half-step between `build/*` (next window) and the learning metrics (window that ended). ~5 MB/run. This replaces `gen_scatter.npz`, whose two fields it contains.

## Considered Options

- **A per-level correlation for metric 3** (extending `eval.py`'s `gencrit_calib_r`). Rejected on two counts. Measured *r* is attenuated by roughly `sqrt(σ²_bodies / (σ²_bodies + σ²_noise/K))`; at level 0 the bodies are identical so `σ²_bodies = 0` and *r* is 0 no matter how good GenCrit is. A per-level curve would therefore climb with distance **purely because the target's signal-to-noise improves** — an artifact reading as "GenCrit generalizes better further out", the reverse of the truth. And correlation is defined to discard bias, while bias *is* the mechanism: over-optimism about unfamiliar designs is what lets a generator wander into bodies that do not work, and pessimism is what pins it to local search. The pooled `gencrit_calib_r` stays valid and is unchanged.
- **Separate skeleton and subtype ladders.** Rejected as scope. Per the `free_entropy` finding the cheap subtype axis moves first, so a joint ladder can accumulate distance without ever changing the body plan — the confound is real. Mitigated rather than removed: each level reports its **skeleton share**, so a flat curve driven entirely by subtype swaps is visible instead of silent.
- **Stochastic ladder rollouts**, matching GenCrit's target distribution. Rejected: it fixes the μ-vs-sampled gap but not staleness, which is the harder of the two, while costing variance and decoupling metric 2 from `eval.py`'s existing numbers. Level-0 anchoring handles both at no cost. Its one assumption — that the offset is flat across levels — is checked once on a single seed by running both action modes, rather than assumed.
- **Recovered level as metric 1.** Rejected: it deliberately ignores resample transients, which is exactly where the PPG and backbone conditions are expected to differ.
- **Mean cross-window `d_struct` as travel.** Rejected: for two *identical* distributions it equals the within-window mean pairwise distance, so a wide static generator scores as fast-moving. Breadth and travel are inseparable in it; energy distance's two subtracted terms are what separate them.
- **Committed-body drift as travel** (`d_struct` between consecutive greedy bodies). Rejected as the headline: argmax is discontinuous, so two near-tied designs trading places registers as large travel with no distributional change, while a large mass shift that does not dethrone the mode registers zero. It also has a nonzero floor by construction, since greedy draws vary with the MDP's random limb ordering.
- **Scalars only, no population dump.** Rejected: travel and coverage are not recoverable from scalars, and any diversity statistic invented later would require re-running every completed experiment.
- **Logging `build/n_modes` during pretrain** so metric 4 spans the full x-axis. Rejected: ADR-0020 needs "whole series" to mean "all RL windows" with no tuner-side arithmetic. Metric 4's panel starts at the boundary and says so.

## Consequences

- **Supersedes [ADR-0015](0015-phase-comparison-methodology.md).** Its phase-comparison harness, scripts and notebooks were deleted with the move to the paper series; the shared measurement code that survived lives in `experiments/harness/`.
- `eval.py`'s `_forward`/`_rollout`/`_install` move into `harness/evalpass.py` and `eval.py` imports them — the ladder needs that exact rollout, and a second copy would be the first violation of the harness's one-implementation-per-metric rule.
- **Metric 1 cannot be read as a control-quality curve**, and any experiment whose story depends on that must say so and point at metric 2.
- **Metric 5 cannot be read as a control-quality number either** — by design it saturates toward the fixed-body ceiling, so a null means the designs were comparable. It is metric 2's opposite, measured on the same checkpoint, and the two are reported together.
- **Metric 5 costs 3 extra waves per condition** (3 checkpoints × 8 seeds, 250 epochs each) — 9 waves for a 3-arm experiment, 12 for a 4-arm one — and requires checkpoints at the three ladder points. For **experiment 2** the strip removes that experiment's own treatment, so its metric 5 measures the aux heads' *legacy* on the representation rather than their continued action; its doc must say so.
- **Metric 5 is the default decision metric, but not a universal one.** Where a condition's cost is accumulated training-dynamics damage rather than a durable difference in representation or design, the 250-epoch single-body fine-tune *repairs the treatment* and metric 5 goes flat by construction. **[Experiment 3](../experiments/clone.md) takes this deviation**: metric 1 at the final window decides, and metric 5 is demoted to the collapse check that catches a metric-1 win bought by locking onto one easy body. Any experiment deviating must name the decision metric in its doc before the runs launch, so the choice cannot be made after seeing which metric moved.
- **Checkpoint cadence is one window, not the config's `save_frequency: 50`.** The ladder and the specialization pass read checkpoints at window boundaries; 50 epochs against 63 lands the nearest save 4–14 epochs into the *following* window, holding one more generator update than its label claims and a partial current window. `harness/launch.py` overrides the cadence to `epochs_per_window`, which also caps what the gym-rebuild crash can cost at one window — the same rule D24 applies on the `codesigner` path. Cost: 48 saves ≈ 620 MB per run.
- **A boundary checkpoint holds the state *after* its boundary.** The resample runs at the end of the epoch and the save follows it, so a checkpoint at epoch `63k` carries `gen_window = k` — generator updated *k* times, window *k*'s bodies already installed. That is the intended meaning of "checkpoint at window *k*", and it is off-by-one-prone in both directions.
- **Warm-starting requires an epoch-budget offset.** A checkpoint carries `epoch_num` and `set_full_state_weights` restores it, while training stops at `epoch_num >= max_epochs` — so a metric-5 pass launched with `max_epochs: 250` off a 3024-epoch checkpoint exits after one epoch. `specialize.py` must set `max_epochs = ckpt_epoch + 250`, which also shifts that run's per-epoch axis and must be un-shifted when scraped. Everything else a warm start needs *is* in the checkpoint: optimizer moments, the observation and value normalizers, and the codesign extras (`gen_window`, the current design arrays, the generation trace).
- **Restoring a checkpoint re-installs its sampled population, and `resample_interval: 0` does not prevent it.** `set_full_state_weights` rebuilds the sim onto the checkpoint's own bodies (`codesign_agent.py:993-996`) so that a resumed run does not earn reward on the seed body — correct for a resume, wrong for specialization, which must then install the **committed** body itself. Left implicit, metric 5 would fine-tune on the window's population and silently measure the wrong thing in every experiment that reports it.
- **A per-epoch scalar axis exists, and it needed a new scalar.** Experiment 3's boundary-recovery trace folds a per-epoch series on the resample boundaries, and rl_games' own `rewards/iter` cannot be that series: it is `game_rewards.get_mean()`, a ring buffer of the last 100 *finished episodes* pooled across envs, so it only moves when episodes end — and at `resample_interval: 1` every env truncates simultaneously at the very instant of the resample. Folded, it yields a fourfold excursion that `episode_lengths/iter` reproduces exactly and that a no-resample run would show identically: it measures episode-completion phase, not re-adaptation. `CodesignAgent` therefore logs **`control/r_step`**, the mean raw (unshaped, un-bootstrapped) reward per env-step over the epoch, accumulated in `env_step` and flushed once per epoch. It must exist before any wave launches, since it is not recoverable afterwards.
- **The scraper owns the conversion between the two x-axes.** `harness/scrape.py` needs an epoch-indexed reader alongside the frame-indexed one — one conversion, in fact, since a frame-indexed tag's epoch is `step/(num_actors·horizon_length) + 1` whether it is written every epoch or every window, and only the `*/iter` mirrors carry the epoch as their step directly. Boundary spacing is exactly `epochs_per_window` with no drift — `_steps_since_resample` resets to zero rather than subtracting, so the per-window overshoot does not accumulate — but the window-cadence scalars are indexed by **frame** and the frame counter lags the epoch by one, so window *k* closes at the end of epoch `63k` and is logged at frame `(63k−1)·num_actors·horizon_length`. Converting between the two axes is the scraper's job and the fold's most likely off-by-one.
- **Experiment 5 (comparison against outside methods) does not fit.** Its rivals have no generator, so metrics 2–4 are undefined for them and its x-axis has no windows. Its protocol is [ADR-0022](0022-cross-method-comparison-protocol.md). Metric 5 is the one metric that crosses over — it needs only a committed body — and is its decision metric too, which is what lets the ablations and the comparison be read against each other.
- **[Experiment 4](../experiments/attention.md) does not fit either, for the opposite reason.** It deliberately runs with `resample_interval: 0` on the canonical base morphology, so *it* has no generator: metrics 2–5 are undefined and metric 1's `quality/R_mean` is never written. It defines its own five measurements in its doc, with asymptotic fixed-body return as the decision metric and its own noise floor (ADR-0018's band was measured with a generator running and does not transfer). This ADR therefore covers experiments 1, 2 and 3.
- **`resample_interval: 0` silently disables LR warmup.** The warmup scheduler is gated on a nonzero interval (`codesign_agent.py:167`), so it never applies to experiment 4 or to any metric-5 specialization pass, which fall back to rl_games' plain `AdaptiveScheduler`. Uniform within a study, so it confounds nothing internally — but returns from a no-resample run are **not** comparable to returns from a resampling one, and no figure may place them on shared axes.
- **FD and FK do not switch off with the generator.** They are per-step control-side terms fused into the PPO loss, so any no-resample run must disable them explicitly or it silently carries experiment 2's treatment. Already true of the metric-5 strip; stated here so it is not rediscovered per study.
- **`scrape.py` must tolerate absent scalar families.** Experiment 4 writes no `quality/*`, `build/*`, `gencrit/*` or `clone/*`. The tolerance belongs in the loader, not in per-study special cases.
- **A resumed run leaves two event files in one summaries dir, with overlapping steps.** `launch.py` resumes crashes from the newest checkpoint (the gym-rebuild race makes that mandatory at 3 h/run), and the resumed process re-runs the epochs between the last save and the crash — so the second file's early steps duplicate the first file's tail. The scraper must prefer the later file where they overlap, rather than averaging or concatenating.
- Runs grow by ~5 MB and gain a `gen_pop/` directory. Artifacts stay gitignored.
